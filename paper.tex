\begin{abstract}
% CONTENIDO: problema concreto, brecha, mecanismo propuesto, evaluación,
% resultados cuantitativos reales y alcance de la conclusión.
% TONO/EXTENSION: un párrafo de 150--250 palabras. Distribución orientativa:
% 2 frases de problema, 2--3 de método y 2 de evidencia y alcance.
% REVISION: novedad reconocible, cifras contextualizadas y correspondencia
% con los resultados. Evitar citas, abreviaturas y ecuaciones desplegadas.
% Redactar al terminar el artículo. El marcador no es un resumen final.
Learned residual dynamics can improve the predictions of a reduced vehicle model, but their usefulness for feedback control depends on the operating condition and on how the correction propagates through the prediction horizon. This article investigates this distinction for lateral trajectory tracking using a physical bicycle model augmented with learned corrections to lateral velocity and yaw rate. The experimental framework combines CarSim with an incremental model predictive controller operating at 50 Hz over a 0.6 s horizon. Deterministic neural, Gaussian-process, and heteroscedastic ensemble residual models provide complementary instruments for studying prediction accuracy, uncertainty, and training-domain coverage. Preliminary closed-loop records illustrate condition-dependent behavior: a neural residual reduces lateral tracking root-mean-square error from 0.2342 to 0.1908 m in a nominal tight double lane change, while increasing it from 0.0765 to 0.9173 m in a double lane change at 50 km/h. These observations motivate a causal interface for estimating residual validity and informing its use in the controller. The proposed evaluation separates prediction error, residual benefit, operational violations, and optimization success, and reserves independent conditions for assessing incremental information from uncertainty and out-of-distribution indicators. The current evidence supports investigating conditional residual use; the effectiveness of the supervisory intervention remains to be established.
\end{abstract}

\subsection{Background and Motivation}
% CONTENIDO: tarea, dominio operativo y consecuencias de la limitación actual.
% TONO/EXTENSION: 1--2 párrafos, de lo general a lo específico.
% REVISION: problema medible. Para T-IV, destacar inteligencia y operación
% automatizada; para TVT, precisar el avance tecnológico vehicular.
Lateral trajectory tracking requires a controller to translate a geometric reference into feasible steering commands despite discrepancies between its prediction model and vehicle dynamics. A reduced bicycle model offers a compact representation for constrained optimization, but changes in speed, tire--road interaction, and vehicle configuration alter the relationship between steering, lateral velocity, and yaw rate. Model predictive control (MPC) makes these discrepancies relevant over an entire sequence of future decisions rather than only at the next sample.

A learned residual can compensate for systematic one-step prediction errors while preserving the physical model as a nominal structure. Its benefit, however, depends on the data supporting the correction and on the states and inputs visited by the controller. A small error on recorded transitions does not establish that the same correction improves a recursively propagated prediction or the resulting feedback trajectory. This study therefore asks when information generated by a learned dynamics model is useful for deciding how that model should influence lateral vehicle control.

\subsection{Related Work and Research Gap}
% CONTENIDO: organizar la literatura por mecanismos y limitaciones.

Learning-based MPC includes approaches that improve the prediction model, learn controller parameters, or combine learning with constraint-handling mechanisms\cite{hewingReview2020}. Additive Gaussian-process models are particularly relevant here: Hewing et al.\ integrate nominal dynamics and a learned nonlinear component, propagating approximate uncertainty into a chance-constrained controller\cite{hewingCautious2019}. That construction illustrates why the uncertainty model and its treatment inside the optimization must be specified together. An uncertainty output alone does not provide the same guarantees.

Vehicle-specific work also combines physical structure and learning. Chen et al.\ study a physics-informed dynamics model and a horizon design for nonlinear predictive trajectory tracking, including real-vehicle experiments\cite{chenExperimentalEvaluationTrajectory2026a}. The present study instead retains a fixed nominal lateral model to examine the conditional usefulness of an additive residual and the information needed to supervise it.

Deep ensembles offer a practical means of estimating predictive uncertainty from multiple networks\cite{lakshminarayanan2017}. In a residual dynamics application, disagreement between predicted means and the predicted conditional variance provide different signals. Neither is identical to distance from the training set, and neither directly measures the future tracking loss of a controller. These quantities must be tested against a defined target rather than used interchangeably as confidence.

The research question addressed here is whether uncertainty, training-domain coverage, and previously observed discrepancies provide incremental information about residual usefulness and impending control degradation. The relevant distinction is between an unfamiliar query, an inaccurate prediction, a correction worse than the physical model, and a closed-loop operational violation. The study does not assume that these events coincide. Establishing novelty relative to the closest recent methods requires a complete, focused literature comparison; the current review identifies the methodological setting rather than claiming that residual supervision is absent from the literature.

\subsection{Main Contributions}

The objective is to characterize the validity of learned residual dynamics for lateral MPC and to evaluate whether that information supports a beneficial supervisory intervention. The manuscript is organized around three contributions under investigation:

\begin{itemize}
  \item A common physical--residual interface that separates one-step accuracy, recursively propagated prediction, and closed-loop utility across deterministic and probabilistic models (Sections~\ref{sec:problem} and~\ref{sec:method}).
  \item A causal validity-assessment formulation using uncertainty, training-support indicators, and past innovation, with explicit ablations against state and reference information. The supervisory rule and its final validation remain pending (Section~\ref{sec:validity}).
  \item A simulation protocol that tests whether prediction improvements transfer to tracking performance under operating-condition changes. Available B0/B1 records provide preliminary evidence of mixed control effects; confirmatory supervision results are still required (Sections~\ref{sec:setup} and~\ref{sec:results}).
\end{itemize}

\label{sec:problem}
% CONTENIDO: sistema, variables, hipótesis, incertidumbre y objetivo formal.

\subsection{Vehicle Model and Notation}
% CONTENIDO: estados, entradas, salidas, dinámica, muestreo y coordenadas.
% TONO/EXTENSION: 2--3 párrafos y las ecuaciones indispensables.
% REVISION: distinguir planta, predictor y estimador; identificar parámetros
% medidos o identificados y definir cada variable antes de utilizarla.
The plant is the CarSim vehicle model coupled to MATLAB/Simulink. The controller uses the path-relative state
\begin{equation}
 x=[e_y,e_\psi,v_y,r]^\top,\qquad
 x_a=[x^\top,\delta_{k-1}]^\top,
\end{equation}
where $e_y$ is lateral displacement error (m), $e_\psi$ is heading error (rad), $v_y$ is lateral velocity (m/s), $r$ is yaw rate (rad/s), and $\delta$ is effective front-wheel steering angle (rad). The manipulated variable is $\Delta\delta_k=\delta_k-\delta_{k-1}$. Longitudinal speed control is separate from lateral control.

For explicit notation, consider positive lateral velocity and steering to the left, positive counterclockwise yaw, and positive reference curvature $\kappa$ for a left turn. With positive axle cornering stiffnesses $C_f,C_r$, vehicle mass $m$, yaw inertia $I_z$, and center-of-mass distances $l_f,l_r$, the linear bicycle equations are
\begin{align}
 \dot e_y&=v_y+v_0e_\psi,\quad \dot e_\psi=r-v_0\kappa,\label{eq:errors}\\
 \dot v_y&=-\frac{C_f+C_r}{mv_0}v_y
 +\frac{C_f}{m}\delta\nonumber\\
 &\quad +\left(\frac{l_rC_r-l_fC_f}{mv_0}-v_0\right)r,\label{eq:vy}\\
 \dot r&=\frac{l_rC_r-l_fC_f}{I_zv_0}v_y
 -\frac{l_f^2C_f+l_r^2C_r}{I_zv_0}r
 +\frac{l_fC_f}{I_z}\delta.\label{eq:r}
\end{align}
These equations state the convention used in the manuscript; signal signs and axle/per-tire stiffness conventions must match the implementation. The nominal matrices are fixed at $v_0=60/3.6$ m/s and discretized with a zero-order hold at $T_s=0.020$ s:
\begin{equation}
 x_{k+1}^{\rm phys}=A_dx_k+B_d\delta_k+E_d\kappa_k.\label{eq:physical}
\end{equation}
Measured $V_x$ is used in the spatial reference preview and as a residual-model input. Its availability does not imply that $A_d$ is updated online. The documented baseline is therefore a fixed-matrix nominal predictor.

\subsection{Operating Domain, Uncertainty, and Assumptions}
% CONTENIDO: velocidades, fricción, carga, sensores, actuadores y entorno.
% TONO/EXTENSION: 1--2 párrafos; enumerar solo supuestos utilizados después.
% REVISION: ruido, retardos, saturación, variables observables y dominio
% de validez. Justificar las cotas utilizadas con datos o referencias.
The identification domain comprises double lane changes (DLC) and tight double lane changes (DLCT) at 60 km/h, nominal friction coefficient $\mu=1$, and the CarSim C-Class hatchback configuration. Evaluation considers changes in speed, road friction, maneuver, and vehicle. These changes may affect both model representativeness and the physical attainability of the reference.

The reduced model assumes small heading deviations in the kinematic error equations, positive nominal longitudinal speed, and linear tire forces. The nominal predictor contains no explicit friction-dependent tire saturation. A residual learned on the available inputs may therefore also represent omitted states and history-dependent effects; it is not an identification of a unique tire law. CarSim states provide simulation measurements. No sensor-noise, state-estimation, or actuator-delay validation is inferred from this configuration.

\subsection{Control Objectives and Constraints}
% CONTENIDO: tarea, referencia, restricciones duras/blandas y criterios de fallo.
% TONO/EXTENSION: 1--2 párrafos y formulación matemática compacta.
% REVISION: objetivos en tensión, factibilidad y disponibilidad de referencia;
% distinguir satisfacción de restricciones y garantías de seguridad.
The control objective is to track the reference while respecting steering-angle and steering-rate limits. The main research question concerns the benefit of a residual correction under a common control configuration. A valid optimization solution is distinguished from successful maneuver completion and from acceptable tracking. The baseline input constraints are
\begin{equation}
 |\delta_k|\leq30^\circ,\qquad
 |\Delta\delta_k|\leq80^\circ\!/{\rm s}\,T_s=1.6^\circ.\label{eq:limits}
\end{equation}
All angular quantities are converted to radians in computation. Operational thresholds for tracking or other vehicle quantities, when used to label events, must be specified independently of these actuator bounds.

\subsection{Overall Architecture}
% CONTENIDO: bloques, interfaces, flujo de información y escalas temporales.
% TONO/EXTENSION: 1--2 párrafos y un diagrama de arquitectura.
% REVISION: diferenciar entrenamiento, inferencia, optimización y planta;

At each sampling instant, measured states and reference information feed the nominal predictor and the residual inference module. An externally generated nominal steering sequence supports recursive residual evaluation over the prediction horizon. The resulting correction sequence is held fixed during each quadratic-program (QP) solve. The optimizer applies only its first steering increment, and the procedure repeats after the next measurement.

The proposed supervisory layer receives causal information from the learned model, the training-support detector, and past discrepancies. It estimates a specified validity or operational-risk target and informs the use of the residual or the horizon configuration. This layer is a research component awaiting a frozen policy and closed-loop validation. Offline residual training, online inference, and future supervisory intervention are separate stages.

\subsection{Learning-Based Prediction Model}
% CONTENIDO: qué se aprende y cómo se integra con el modelo nominal.
% TONO/EXTENSION: 2--4 párrafos distribuidos entre los subapartados.
% REVISION: causalidad temporal, cobertura de datos y tratamiento del error.
All residual models use the query and target
\begin{align}
 z_k&=[V_{x,k},v_{y,k},r_k,\delta_k]^\top,\\
 d_k&=x_{{\rm lat},k+1}^{\rm CarSim}
       -x_{{\rm lat},k+1|k}^{\rm phys},\qquad
 x_{\rm lat}=[v_y,r]^\top.\label{eq:target}
\end{align}
The physical one-step prediction is initialized at the measured state and uses the same effective steering and sample interval as the transition being evaluated. With
$S=[\begin{smallmatrix}0&0\\0&0\\1&0\\0&1\end{smallmatrix}]$, the hybrid prediction is
\begin{equation}
 \hat x_{k+1|k}=A_dx_k+B_d\delta_k+E_d\kappa_k
                      +S\hat d_\theta(z_k).\label{eq:hybrid}
\end{equation}
The targets are discrete state corrections in m/s and rad/s, not continuous-time derivatives. They are therefore injected without an additional multiplication by $T_s$. Although the direct correction affects only $v_y,r$, its influence on tracking errors propagates through subsequent prediction steps.

\subsubsection{Offline Identification and Training}

The original identification set contains 3102 training, 1034 validation, and 1034 nominal test transitions, separated by complete simulation runs. Input and output normalization use training statistics only. One-step transitions are the regression targets; accumulated rollout errors and maneuver-activity labels are not additional fitting inputs. Table~\ref{tab:models} summarizes the model roles.

B1 uses MATLAB \texttt{fitrnet}, with seed 42 in the recovered training record and validation-based selection between iteration budgets of 100 and 200. The selected saved configuration must be read from the trained artifact. B2 fits separate scalar models for the two residual outputs, using a constant mean basis, exact fitting and prediction, and external normalization rather than internal standardization.

B3 is the principal candidate for uncertainty-based supervision. Each member estimates two means and two conditional variances. For a Gaussian conditional likelihood, the per-sample objective takes the form
\begin{equation}
 \mathcal L=\frac12\sum_{q\in\{v_y,r\}}
 \left[\log(2\pi\sigma_q^2(z))+

\subsubsection{Online Inference and Uncertainty Handling}
% CONTENIDO: ventana histórica, incertidumbre, actualización y latencia.

Residual parameters are treated as frozen during inference; online learning is not part of the recovered baseline. For B3, let $\mu_m(z)$ and $\sigma_m^2(z)$ be a member's mean and variance for one output. The mixture moments are
\begin{align}
 \bar\mu(z)&=M^{-1}\sum_{m=1}^{M}\mu_m(z),\quad M=5,\\
 U_{\rm ale}(z)&=M^{-1}\sum_m\sigma_m^2(z),\\
 U_{\rm epi}(z)&=M^{-1}\sum_m(\mu_m(z)-\bar\mu(z))^2,\\
 U_{\rm tot}(z)&=U_{\rm ale}(z)+U_{\rm epi}(z).\label{eq:uq}
\end{align}
Here $U$ denotes variance; standard deviations are its square roots. These equations define the manuscript's reporting convention and must be reconciled with exported fields. Ensemble disagreement is an epistemic proxy, and predicted conditional variance is an aleatoric proxy. With deterministic simulation and a reduced input representation, the latter can absorb unresolved dynamics and model mismatch; it is not evidence of purely physical randomness.

Baseline hybrid MPC uses the residual mean. Uncertainty is assessed separately and does not automatically introduce chance constraints or robustness guarantees. Calibration is evaluated against observed prediction errors, separately for each output and condition.

\subsection{Predictive Control Formulation}
% CONTENIDO: optimización completa, horizonte y política de aplicación.
% TONO/EXTENSION: 2--3 párrafos, ecuaciones alineadas y símbolos definidos.
% REVISION: coherencia entre predictor, coste, restricciones y solver.
With the augmented state, define
\begin{align}
 A_a&=\begin{bmatrix}A_d&B_d\\0&1\end{bmatrix},\quad
 B_a=\begin{bmatrix}B_d\\1\end{bmatrix},\nonumber\\
 E_a&=\begin{bmatrix}E_d\\0\end{bmatrix},\quad
 S_a=\begin{bmatrix}S\\0_{1\times2}\end{bmatrix}.
\end{align}
The predicted recursion is
\begin{equation}
 \hat x_{a,j+1|k}=A_a\hat x_{a,j|k}+B_a\Delta\delta_{j|k}
           +E_a\kappa_{j|k}+S_a\bar d_{j|k},\label{eq:aug}
\end{equation}
initialized with $\hat x_{a,0|k}=x_{a,k}$. The sequence $\bar d_{j|k}$ is obtained by recursive rollout along the nominal steering sequence, starting from measured lateral states, and frozen for optimization. Stacking gives
\begin{equation}
 X=F x_{a,k}+G\Delta U+G_\kappa K+G_dD.\label{eq:stack}
\end{equation}
For $N_p=N_c=30$, $X\in\R^{150}$, $\Delta U\in\R^{30}$, $D\in\R^{60}$, and $G_d\in\R^{150\times60}$. Each pair in $D$ is ordered as lateral-velocity and yaw-rate correction. The finite-horizon problem minimizes the cost below subject to~\eqref{eq:aug} and~\eqref{eq:limits}. Setting $D=0$ recovers B0.

The learned model is generally nonlinear in its query, but freezing $D$ makes it an additive prediction term in a QP. The residual is not re-evaluated at every candidate optimization input. This approximation and the nominal-sequence construction are therefore part of the controller definition.

\subsection{Causal Validity and Risk Assessment}
\label{sec:validity}
Three targets are kept distinct. Accuracy validity concerns whether a prediction error exceeds an application-specific tolerance. Residual utility concerns whether the hybrid prediction is worse than the physical prediction on the same transition or input replay. Operational risk concerns an upcoming violation in the controlled plant. A single score cannot be interpreted as all three without separate validation.

For standardized inputs $\tilde z$, training-support indicators include nearest-neighbor distance and regularized Mahalanobis distance:
\begin{align}
 s_{\rm NN}(z)&=\min_{i\in\mathcal I_{\rm train}}
          \|\tilde z-\tilde z_i\|_2,\\
 s_{\rm Mah}(z)&=\sqrt{(\tilde z-\bar z)^\top
                  (\Sigma_z+\lambda I)^{-1}(\tilde z-\bar z)}.
\end{align}
The $K$th-neighbor distance, including the investigated $K=10$, provides an alternative support score. Training statistics, neighbor selection, and $\lambda$ are frozen before final testing. These scores quantify input-space support rather than friction or prediction error. Because training uses nearly constant speed, speed scaling and near-singular covariance directions require explicit treatment; an arbitrary near-zero standard deviation must not silently dominate the score.

A causal innovation becomes available only after the corresponding transition has completed:
\begin{equation}
 \nu_{k-1}=x_{{\rm lat},k}^{\rm CarSim}
   -\hat x_{{\rm lat},k|k-1}^{\rm hyb}.
\end{equation}
Candidate validity features combine current state/reference information, $U_{\rm epi}$, $U_{\rm ale}$, support scores, and innovations available through time $k$. The residual target $d_k$ requires $x_{k+1}$ and cannot be an online feature at time $k$.

For a chosen operational admissible set $\mathcal A$, a prospective event label can be written as
\begin{equation}
 F_{k,H}=\mathbf 1\{\exists j\in\{1,\ldots,H\}:
                   \eta_{k+j}\notin\mathcal A\},\label{eq:event}
\end{equation}
where $\eta$ contains the selected operational variables. Labels are built offline. Proposed initial values of 0.6 s anticipation ($H=30$) and 0.4 s history (20 samples) are development choices, not validated settings. Already active violations, incomplete future windows, and early termination need explicit labeling rules. A score is called a probability only after calibration has been evaluated.

\subsection{Supervisory Interface Under Investigation}
The intended supervisory contribution is the mapping from causal indicators to a control-relevant decision. A candidate residual-use interface is
\begin{equation}
 \bar d_{j|k}=\alpha_{j|k}\hat d_\theta(\hat z^{\rm nom}_{j|k}),
 \qquad 0\leq\alpha_{j|k}\leq1.
\end{equation}
Here $\alpha=1$ gives the always-active residual and $\alpha=0$ the physical predictor. This equation defines a possible interface, not a validated switching policy. Adapting residual depth at fixed $N_p$ differs from changing the optimization horizon.

The alternative horizon study considers $N_p\in\{15,30,45\}$ with a common $N_c=15$ across its comparators. Its 30/15 baseline is a new configuration and cannot be replaced by a historical 30/30 run. One intervention branch must be selected after the prediction-depth diagnosis; the manuscript does not presume that lower validity should shorten the horizon. Policy thresholds, hysteresis and minimum dwell time must be frozen on development conditions.

The evaluation addresses five hypotheses: H1, the residual adds value in part of the domain; H2, its indicators add anticipation beyond states and references; H3, the predictor retains utility in reserved conditions; H4, the procedure can be repeated with another identification dataset; and H5, supervision reduces operational violations. The present results motivate H1 and the need for H2--H5, but do not establish their confirmatory outcomes.

The current evidence is limited to simulation and exploratory historical comparisons.
